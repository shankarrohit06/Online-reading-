Reading Pencil

Read online the way you read on paper: lines under the text, a pencil on the word you're on, and a voice that reads along with you.

Get started

  1. Pin it. Click the puzzle-piece icon at the top right of Chrome, then the pin next to Reading Pencil, so its icon is always in the toolbar.

  2. Switch it on for a site. Open an article and press Alt+R or click the icon. Each site remembers its setting.

  3. Follow along. Point at a word and it grows a little. Alt+← → moves word by word, Alt+↑ ↓ line by line.

  4. Listen. Select some text and click the speaker, or press Alt+S. While it reads, click any word to jump there. Esc stops.

Practice here

Reading Pencil is always on for this page. Try the pencil, the keys and read aloud on the article below.

Why a pencil helps you read

When you read a printed page, you can run a pencil under each line and keep your place one word at a time. Your eyes stop drifting, and long paragraphs become far easier to follow from start to finish.

Screens take that anchor away. Text scrolls, lines stretch across wide windows, and it is easy to skip a line or read the same one twice. Hearing the words while you watch them helps them stick.

Select this paragraph and click the speaker that appears beside it. The pencil will move with the voice, and the sentence being read is tinted so you can see what's coming.

Voices

Play a voice to hear it, then choose the one you like. Word by word voices keep the pencil exactly on the word being spoken.

Other languages

Chrome found no voices. On Windows and macOS they come from your system's speech settings; on ChromeOS and Linux, Chrome's own Google voices appear when you're online.
